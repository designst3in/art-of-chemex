# Research Brief — Art of Chemex

*Compressed public-web research for workshop / Docsify course pages. Prefer primary sources; conflicting claims flagged. Do not treat secondary paraphrases as quotes.*

## Executive summary

The Chemex is a one-piece borosilicate pour-over invented by German-American chemist **Dr. Peter Schlumbohm** (1896–1962). The foundational U.S. utility patent is **US 2,241,368** (“Filtering device”), filed **13 Apr 1939**, granted **6 May 1941**. The familiar hourglass — upper ~60° funnel + Erlenmeyer-like lower flask, with an internal air/pour groove — adapts laboratory glassware to the home kitchen. MoMA put it on the cover of its **Useful Objects in Wartime** bulletin (Dec 1942–Jan 1943) and accessioned an example as **51.1943**. Thicker **bonded** paper filters remove more oils and fines than typical drip paper, yielding high clarity and lighter body — hence its specialty-coffee second life.

**Confidence:** High on patent US2241368, MoMA accession 51.1943, Useful Objects wartime cover, and filter geometry. Medium on biography dates (1935 vs 1936 immigration; IIT “100 best” year 1956 vs 1958). **Avoid:** any claim that **US2399935** is Chemex (it is not).
