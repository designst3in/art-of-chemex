# 01 · History & Impact

## Snapshot

- **Object:** Chemex pour-over coffee maker
- **Inventor:** Dr. Peter Schlumbohm (1896–1962), German-American chemist
- **Core patent:** [US 2,241,368](https://patents.google.com/patent/US2241368A/en) “Filtering device” — filed **1939-04-13**, granted **1941-05-06**
- **Market introduction:** **1941** (hourglass borosilicate vessel; wood collar + tie follow in production)
- **Museum landmark:** MoMA *Useful Objects in Wartime* (Dec 1942–Jan 1943) — Chemex on the bulletin cover; permanent collection accession **51.1943**

## Teaching arc

Lab glassware thinking → wartime “good design” → mid-century icon → specialty-coffee revival.

1. **Invention (1938–1941).** German priority **1938-04-13**; U.S. filing **1939-04-13**; grant **1941-05-06**. Chemex Corp. incorporated in New York **1939**.
2. **Wartime design culture (1942–1943).** MoMA exhibition *[Useful Objects in Wartime Under $10](https://www.moma.org/calendar/exhibitions/2733)* (2 Dec 1942 – 9 Jan 1943). Bulletin lists Chemex at **$6.50** (Lewis & Conger); cover image in the [wartime bulletin PDF](https://assets.moma.org/documents/moma_catalogue_2733_300164646.pdf).
3. **Collection (1943).** MoMA accession **[51.1943](https://www.moma.org/collection/works/1847)** — gift of Lewis & Conger.
4. **Mid-century recognition.** IIT “100 best-designed products of modern times” — year reported as **1956** (Lemelson) or **1958** (Wikipedia / Chemex site) — present both.
5. **Design patent (1944).** [USD 137,943](https://patents.google.com/patent/USD137943S/en) “Combined flask and handle” — filed 1943-01-12, issued **1944-05-23** (likely what Cooper Hewitt means by “patented 1944”).
6. **Later utility patents.** Filter-paper geometry [US 2,693,883](https://patents.google.com/patent/US2693883A/en) (issued 1954); [US 2,897,970](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/2897970) (1959); also US2411340 decanter flask (1946).
7. **Afterlife.** Schlumbohm dies **1962**. From the 2000s onward, specialty coffee rediscovers the thick bonded filter and high-clarity cup; still produced in Massachusetts.
