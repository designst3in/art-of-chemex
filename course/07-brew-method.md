# 07 · Brew Method

## Goal

A repeatable Chemex brew for the workshop — clear enough for beginners, flexible for coffee nerds. Design + science first (Modules 01–06); then hands-on ritual.

House recipes below are **canonical** for the approved 90-min run of show ([08 · Workshop Run of Show](08-workshop-run-of-show.md)).

## House recipes (workshop)

### Hot — usual 3-pour

| Parameter | Value | Notes |
|-----------|-------|-------|
| Coffee | **40 g** | Medium-coarse (sea salt); adjust to drawdown |
| Water | **640 ml** (~640 g) | Ratio **1:16** |
| Method | Usual **3-pour** | Bloom + two follow-on pours |
| Water temp | Just off boil | ~**92–96 °C** typical teaching range |
| Total time | ~3.5–4.5 min | Guide, not law |
| Filter | Chemex bonded | Multi-layer fold toward spout |

**Teaching sequence (hot):**

1. Fold filter; place in upper cone (thicker layers toward the pouring spout / groove side).
2. Rinse with hot water; discard rinse (preheats vessel; washes paper).
3. Add **40 g** grounds; level the bed.
4. **Pour 1 — Bloom:** pour ~2× coffee weight (~80 g); wait ~30–45 s.
5. **Pour 2 & 3:** continue in concentric circles / pulses to **640 ml** total.
6. Allow drain; remove filter; swirl; serve from spout using the wood collar as handle.

### Ice — Stumptown-style on ice

| Parameter | Value | Notes |
|-----------|-------|-------|
| Coffee | **24 g** | Coarse enough for Chemex drawdown on ice |
| Ice | **200 g** | In the Chemex carafe (under the filter) |
| Water | **200 g** | Hot brew water poured over grounds |
| Method | Flash / ice Chemex | Hot brew onto ice — not immersion cold brew |
| In-session demo | **Assemble + explain** | Do **not** run a full steep in the timed demo |
| External video | Stumptown Chemex ice / cold brew | Link to be confirmed before teaching day |

**Teaching sequence (ice demo):**

1. Stage **200 g** ice in the Chemex carafe.
2. Set filter + **24 g** grounds as for hot brew (rinse filter *before* ice is in play, or use a dry setup per instructor preference).
3. Explain bloom + slow pour to **200 g** hot water onto the bed so brew drips onto ice.
4. Point participants to the external Stumptown-linked video for full method / timing at home.
5. Buffer block (60–90) is the place for optional redo / tasting if ice and time allow.

## Why the filter matters (science beat)

Bonded Chemex paper is thicker than many basket drip papers. It tends to retain more **oils** and **fines**, which participants often describe as a **cleaner**, lighter-bodied cup. Use as a sensory hypothesis for the room — not dogma.

Connect back to patent language: paper cone + conical vessel as a deliberate filtration system (US2241368; filter blank US2693883).

## Sensory teaching notes

- Expect clarity relative to metal-filter methods.
- Defects to demo: channeling (uneven bed), grind too fine (stall), grind too coarse (under-extraction / thin / sour).
- Ice cup: brighter / thinner body is normal for flash-chill; contrast with hot 3-pour in tasting.
- Align sensory language with any cupping sheet used in the room.
